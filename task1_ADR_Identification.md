Task 1: Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) Identification Basics

## 1. Case Summary
* **Patient Context:** Patient initiated on antibiotic therapy (Amoxicillin) for an underlying infection.
* **Timeline of Events:**
  - Day 0: Amoxicillin initiated.
  - Day 3–5: Onset of skin rash / pruritus / urticarial lesions following drug administration.
  - Continuation: Symptoms persist while medication is continued.
* **Suspected Medication:** Amoxicillin
* **Reported Adverse Event:** Cutaneous adverse reaction / Hypersensitivity reaction (Type I / Type IV).


## 2. Causality Assessment (WHO-UMC & Naranjo Scale)

### WHO-UMC Causality Criteria:
* **Assessment Result:** **Probable / Likely**
* **Justification:**
  - Plausible time sequence between drug administration and event onset.
  - The reaction cannot be reasonably explained by the patient’s concurrent disease.
  - Clinical response to withdrawal (de-challenge) or persistence upon continuation supports drug involvement.

### Naranjo Algorithm Evaluation:
1. Previous conclusive reports on this reaction? (+1)
2. Did the adverse event appear after the suspected drug was administered? (+2)
3. Did the adverse reaction improve when the drug was discontinued or a specific antagonist was administered? (+1)
4. Did the adverse reaction reappear when the drug was readministered? (0 – Not rechallenged)
5. Are there alternative causes that could on their own have caused the reaction? (-1 / 0)
6. Did the reaction appear when a placebo was given? (0)
7. Was the drug detected in the blood in concentrations known to be toxic? (0)
8. Was the reaction more severe when the dose was increased, or less severe when the dose was decreased? (0)
9. Did the patient have a similar reaction to the same or similar compounds in any previous exposure? (+1)
10. Was the adverse event confirmed by any objective evidence? (+1)

•	**Total Score:** ~5 to 6 (Indicates a **Probable** ADR).


## 3. Pharmacovigilance & Regulatory Action Plan
1. **Immediate Patient Safety:** Advise immediate discontinuation of suspected Amoxicillin under medical supervision and initiate supportive antihistamines/corticosteroids if clinically indicated.
2. **Documentation & Reporting:** 
   - Complete the **Suspected Adverse Drug Reaction Reporting Form** (PvPI / CDSCO).
   - Capture minimum required elements: Identifiable Patient, Identifiable Reporter, Suspected Drug, and Adverse Event.
   - Upload details via the **ADR PvPI Mobile App** or submit to the nearest ADR Monitoring Centre (AMC).
